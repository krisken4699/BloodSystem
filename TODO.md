# Blood System — TODO

## Wound soaking and bleeding down

What it should look like. No implementation detail here on purpose.

### The idea

Shoot a sosig and the blood shouldn't just come out of the wound and fly off. It
should also soak into whatever it lands on — the shirt, the armour, the skin —
and keep spreading after the shot.

### On a clothed or armoured sosig

- Red soaks into the fabric around the entry wound, a circle spreading outward
  from the hole.
- It keeps growing slowly rather than appearing at full size.
- At the same time it starts running downward, so the circle grows a tail.
- Final shape is an upside-down teardrop: round at the wound, tapering as it
  runs down.
- It should read as fabric drinking the blood, not as a sticker placed on it.

### On bare skin, no shirt

- Nothing soaks, because there's nothing to soak into. The blood sits on the body.
- Instead of a wide stain it runs — a narrow, clearer trickle, like a small river
  down the skin.
- Not the current look of a blood droplet popping out of the wound. It should
  flow.
- The trickle keeps going. It runs off one part of the body and continues onto the
  next instead of stopping where the body bends.

### Which way it runs

- Always the way gravity pulls, not the way the body is pointing.
- Standing sosig: blood runs down its chest and legs.
- Sosig lying on the ground: blood runs down its side toward the floor.

### How the blood itself should look

- Not solid red. Soft-edged.
- Even the middle isn't fully opaque — light should still come through it, the way
  a wet patch looks rather than paint.
- It has to sit ON the curve of the armour and follow it. A flat image pasted onto
  a round chest plate is exactly what this shouldn't look like.
